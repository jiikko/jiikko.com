Many Macs,
one window.

SyakeScreen is a screen sharing client that shows many Macs on your network side by side and lets you control the one you pick right there. It stays connected; pick one large Mac, up to four tiles, or a gallery. Nothing to install on the other Macs — just turn on the built-in Screen Sharing.

Turn on System Settings > General > Sharing > Screen Sharing on each Mac you want to reach.

Features

Built for working with several Macs at once.

  • See and control many Macs at once — one Mac large / up to four tiles / a gallery with one large Mac and the rest below. Control the selected Mac right in the layout
  • Stays connected — register as many Macs as you like and they connect on launch. Macs you are not looking at stop drawing to save CPU
  • The same high-performance mode as the Mac's own — 60 fps low-latency video and the remote Mac's audio. Falls back to Standard (TCP) on networks that block UDP
  • Nothing to install on the other Mac — just turn on the built-in Screen Sharing
  • Remembers your setup — Macs, passwords (in the Keychain), video mode, layout, names you gave, and window positions and sizes. Launch and pick up where you left off. One window for all your Macs, not one per connection
  • Switch what you see instantly — ⌃⌥1–9 in list order. Move the remote Mac's Spaces (desktops and full-screen apps) with ⌃← ⌃→
  • Keyboard and clipboard — ⌘C and ⌘V work on the remote Mac. Text and URL clipboard sharing. ABC / Japanese input switching is mirrored
  • See how the connection is doing — fps, bitrate, queueing delay and loss on screen and in a diagnostics window. Tells you why the picture stopped
  • Separate windows and full screen — pop out just the Mac you want into its own window or full screen. Position, size and full-screen state are remembered per Mac

Requirements

  • macOS 26 or later / Apple Silicon (M1 or later) only
  • Screen Sharing turned on on the other Mac (System Settings > General > Sharing)
  • For High Performance, UDP 5900–5902 must pass between the Macs (use Standard on VPNs or networks that block UDP)

Privacy

Passwords are stored only in your Mac's Keychain. Screen images, audio, input and the clipboard travel only between your Mac and the Mac you connect to. No analytics, advertising or tracking.
